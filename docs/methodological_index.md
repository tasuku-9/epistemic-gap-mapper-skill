# Methodological Index

The methodological index tags the kind of reasoning used by a claim or comparison.

## M1 — Abductive Reasoning

Inference to a candidate best explanation across multiple observations. It does not prove the hypothesis.

## M2 — Claim Stratification

The A/B/C/X/M system for separating evidence, plausible inference, speculation, overclaim, and method.

## M3 — Consilience

The convergence of independent lines of evidence onto a shared explanatory frame.

## M4 — Myth as Social Memory, Not Literal Chronicle

Myths are not read as direct historical records. Persistent narrative structures, toponyms, and depictions of ritual change may be examined for possible correspondences.

## M5 — Cautionary Myth-Archaeology Analogy

Examples such as Troy/Schliemann may illustrate the danger and potential of myth-guided inquiry. They should be treated cautiously.

## M6 — Oral Genealogy and Political Legitimacy

Examples such as the Hawaiian Kumulipo show how oral cosmological genealogies can bind creation, descent, ecology, and political legitimacy without being literal chronicles.

## M7 — Heuristic Comparative History

Comparative cases such as highland/lowland conflict, imperial absorption, or ritual incorporation are used as possibility structures, not evidence.

## M8 — Falsification-Oriented Framing

A claim must state what evidence would weaken or falsify it.

## M9 — Target-Paper Mapping

A new hypothesis should specify how it accepts, extends, qualifies, or contradicts target papers.

## M10 — Narrative Audit

A new hypothesis should distinguish conflict with a specific paper from conflict with a diffuse standard narrative.
