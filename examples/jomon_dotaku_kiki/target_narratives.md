# Target Narratives for Example Case

## Narrative N1 — Yayoi-over-Jomon demographic overlay

Modern Japanese population history is usually narrated as Yayoi agriculturalists overlaying Jomon hunter-gatherers, with Jomon ancestry surviving as residual ancestry.

- Source status: partly paper-supported, partly textbook-canonical.
- Citation debt: medium.
- Hypothesis relation: accepts admixture/overlay but asks whether paternal asymmetry has social-historical meaning.

## Narrative N2 — Y-D as passive Jomon survival

D1a2a/D-M55 persistence is typically treated as passive Jomon-associated survival rather than a signal of social advantage.

- Source status: synthetic consensus / AI-frequency narrative.
- Citation debt: medium to high.
- Hypothesis relation: reframes this as potentially sex-biased social structure.

## Narrative N3 — Dotaku discontinuity as internal ritual transformation

Dotaku disappearance is often narrated as cultural transformation or transition into new ritual forms.

- Source status: textbook-canonical / review-supported.
- Citation debt: medium.
- Hypothesis relation: does not deny internal transformation, but tests social reorganization and network disruption as possible factors.

## Narrative N4 — Kiki as royal legitimation only

Kiki myths are often treated as royal legitimation narratives rather than possible strata of social memory.

- Source status: review-supported / disciplinary habit.
- Citation debt: medium.
- Hypothesis relation: does not read Kiki literally; asks whether recurring structures may encode ritual-political memory.
