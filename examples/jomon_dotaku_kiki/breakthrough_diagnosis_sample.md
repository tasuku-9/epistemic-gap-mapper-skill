## Why

The manuscript does not primarily contradict the main target genetic or archaeological papers. It challenges a diffuse standard narrative that treats Jomon-associated paternal survival, dotaku discontinuity, and Kiki mythic structures as unrelated phenomena.

## A-level Foundations

- D1a2a/D-M55 persists at high frequency in modern Japanese male lineages.
- Dotaku ritual flourished and then ended in the Late Yayoi period.
- Kiki texts contain narratives of descent, land creation, land cession, and agricultural-origin motifs.

## B-level Contributions

- Paternal asymmetry may be examined as a possible social-historical signal.
- Dotaku discontinuity may be examined as a possible sign of ritual-political reorganization or network disruption.
- Kiki mythic structures may be examined as possible strata of ritual-political memory.

## C-level Vulnerabilities

- Specific mechanisms of social advantage.
- Whether military conflict was involved.
- Direct causal links between continental genetic changes and Yayoi formation.
- Specific myth-to-site correspondences.

## X-level Problems to Avoid

- “The dotaku sphere was immigrant” without qualification.
- “Kiki records these events” as direct history.
- “Comparative cases prove the Japanese case.”
- “Y-chromosome data proves conquest.”

## Required Patches

- Use “continental-associated Dotaku cultural sphere” rather than “immigrant Dotaku cultural sphere.”
- Use “may indicate social advantage” rather than “demonstrates dominance.”
- Use “may reflect structures of ritual-political memory” rather than “records historical events.”
- Mark comparative cases as heuristic analogies.

## Falsification Plan

- Obtain ancient DNA from Yayoi and Kofun elite and non-elite burials.
- Improve regional dotaku chronology.
- Test whether dotaku discontinuity is better explained by internal cultural transformation.
- Examine whether Kiki structures can be explained entirely by standard mythological typology without historical-social residue.
