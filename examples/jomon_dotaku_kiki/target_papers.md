# Target Papers for Example Case

| Citation | Role | Relation to hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| Hanihara 1991 | Canonical Frame Paper | Establishes the dual-structure baseline. The hypothesis does not simply reject it; it asks whether paternal asymmetry complicates the narrative. |
| Cooke et al. 2021 | Data-Anomaly / Updated Frame Paper | Supports tripartite ancestry modeling. The hypothesis accepts this and asks about social-historical implications of paternal asymmetry. |
| Liu et al. 2024 | Data-Anomaly / Updated Frame Paper | Supports updated Japanese ancestry modeling. Used as background for moving beyond a simple dual model. |
| Hammer et al. 2006 | Data Paper | Supports dual origins in Y-chromosome lineages. Does not prove social dominance. |
| Sato et al. 2014 | Data Paper | Supports Y-chromosome frequency observations. Does not prove conquest. |
| Inoue & Sato 2024 | Data Paper | Updates Japanese Y-haplogroup classifications and frequencies. Useful for distinguishing O-lineages. |
| Hudson 1992 | Bridge Paper | Provides archaeological/ritual context for dotaku. Does not prove external disruption. |
| Mabuchi & Hirao 1982 | Evidence Paper | Supports lead isotope analysis of dotaku raw material provenance. |
| Fujio 2015 | Chronology Paper / Book | Provides AMS-based long chronology for Yayoi. |
| Kojiki / Nihon Shoki | Primary Texts | Provide mythic structures. Not direct historical chronicles. |
