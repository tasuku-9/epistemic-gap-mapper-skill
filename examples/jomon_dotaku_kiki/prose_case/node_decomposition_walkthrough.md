# Node Decomposition Walkthrough: Jomon / Dotaku / Kiki Case

This walkthrough shows how the prose-only working paper can be decomposed into Epistemic Gap Mapper nodes. The goal is not to prove the hypothesis. The goal is to identify what is evidence, what is inference, what is speculative, what is a narrative conflict, and what would weaken the model.

## 1. Central Hypothesis

**CH1 — Central Hypothesis**

Jomon-associated paternal lineages may have gained some form of social advantage during the Late Yayoi to early Kofun transition, and this process may help connect three otherwise separate phenomena: paternal-lineage asymmetry, dotaku ritual discontinuity, and Kiki mythic structures involving descent, land transfer, agricultural origins, and ritual-political legitimacy.

Tier: **B**

Reason: The hypothesis integrates existing observations without claiming direct proof. It remains abductive and testable.

## 2. A-level Foundation Nodes

**E1 — D1a2a/D-M55 persistence**

Modern Japanese male populations show substantial persistence of D1a2a/D-M55, often treated as a Jomon-associated paternal lineage.

Node type: Evidence Node  
Tier: **A**  
Target papers: Sato et al. 2014; Inoue & Sato 2024  
Risk: The frequency itself does not prove political dominance.

**E2 — Autosomal continental-derived ancestry**

Most modern mainland Japanese populations have a large share of continental-derived autosomal ancestry, though regional variation is important.

Node type: Evidence Node  
Tier: **A**  
Target papers: Cooke et al. 2021; Liu et al. 2024  
Risk: Regional variation must not be flattened.

**E3 — Dotaku discontinuity**

Dotaku bronze-bell rituals flourished in western Japan and later ended, with bells buried in ritual contexts.

Node type: Evidence Node  
Tier: **A**  
Target papers: Hudson 1992; dotaku archaeology literature  
Risk: Discontinuity does not automatically imply conquest.

**E4 — Kiki mythic complexes**

The Kojiki and Nihon Shoki contain mythic complexes involving heavenly descent, land creation, land cession or submission, and violent agricultural origin.

Node type: Evidence Node  
Tier: **A**  
Primary sources: Kojiki; Nihon Shoki  
Risk: Mythic presence does not equal historical record.

## 3. B-level Inference Nodes

**I1 — Paternal asymmetry as possible social signal**

The asymmetry between continental-derived autosomal ancestry and Jomon-associated paternal-line persistence may indicate some form of social or reproductive advantage.

Node type: Inference Node  
Tier: **B**  
Supported by: E1, E2  
Risk: Alternative explanations include drift, founder effects, later demographic processes, social institutions, or sampling structure.

**I2 — Dotaku discontinuity as possible social reorganization**

The end of the dotaku ritual may reflect social or political reorganization, network disruption, or ritual authority transfer.

Node type: Inference Node  
Tier: **B**  
Supported by: E3  
Risk: Internal ritual transformation may be sufficient.

**I3 — Kiki structures as possible ritual-political memory**

Recurring Kiki structures of descent, land transfer, agricultural origin, and submission may preserve transformed social memory of political or ritual integration.

Node type: Inference Node  
Tier: **B**  
Supported by: E4  
Method tag: Myth as social memory, not literal chronicle  
Risk: Mythological motifs may be explained typologically without local historical memory.

## 4. C-level Vulnerability Nodes

**C1 — Military conquest mechanism**

Jomon-associated groups may have gained advantage partly through military pressure or conquest.

Node type: User Claim Node  
Tier: **C**  
Risk: No direct archaeological or genetic proof yet.  
Required wording: “possibly,” “among several mechanisms,” “not yet assertable.”

**C2 — Direct continental political-event bridge**

Population pressures around the Shang-Zhou transition may have contributed to broader coastal movements related to Yayoi formation.

Node type: User Claim Node  
Tier: **C**  
Risk: Direct causal chain from a specific political event to Yayoi migration is not demonstrated.

**C3 — Specific myth-to-site correspondence**

Specific Kiki place names or deities may correspond to particular archaeological sites or historical groups.

Node type: User Claim Node  
Tier: **C**  
Risk: This is not established and should not be written as a direct identification.

## 5. X-level Overclaim Risks

**X1 — “immigrant Dotaku cultural sphere”**

This wording implies that dotaku communities themselves are proven immigrant populations.

Node type: Risk / Overclaim Node  
Tier: **X**  
Patch: Use “continental-associated Dotaku cultural sphere” or “continental-associated Yayoi/Dotaku cultural sphere.”

**X2 — Myth as direct record**

Any wording that says the Kiki “records” a specific event risks mythic literalism.

Node type: Risk / Overclaim Node  
Tier: **X**  
Patch: Use “may preserve transformed structures of social memory” or “may reflect ritual-political memory.”

**X3 — Comparative examples as evidence**

Comparisons to highland/lowland conquests, Hainuwele, Kumulipo, or Schliemann/Troy are not evidence for the Japanese case.

Node type: Risk / Overclaim Node  
Tier: **X if used as evidence; M if used as heuristic analogy**  
Patch: Attach edge type `heuristic_analogy_for`, not `supports`.

## 6. Target Narrative Nodes

**N1 — Passive Jomon survival narrative**

Jomon-associated paternal lineages are often treated as survivals or residual ancestry rather than possible signals of social dominance.

Node type: Narrative Node  
Source status: partly paper-supported, partly textbook-canonical, possibly AI-frequency narrative  
Challenge type: narrative integration, not direct data contradiction.

**N2 — Dotaku internal transformation narrative**

Dotaku discontinuity is often treated as internal ritual or cultural transformation.

Node type: Narrative Node  
Source status: review-supported / disciplinary habit  
Challenge type: alternative explanatory bridge, not direct rejection.

**N3 — Kiki as royal legitimation only**

Kiki myths are often treated primarily as royal legitimation narratives rather than strata of social memory.

Node type: Narrative Node  
Source status: disciplinary habit / review-supported  
Challenge type: reframing, not direct contradiction.

## 7. Falsifier Nodes

**F1 — Kofun elite ancient DNA**

If sufficiently representative Kofun-period elite burials do not show elevated D1a2a/D-M55 relative to non-elite or contemporaneous populations, the paternal-elite version of the hypothesis is weakened.

Node type: Falsifier Node  
Targets: I1, CH1

**F2 — Dotaku chronology without network disruption**

If regional dotaku chronologies fully explain the ritual discontinuity as internal transformation, without evidence of broader social or network-level disruption, the dotaku bridge is weakened.

Node type: Falsifier Node  
Targets: I2, CH1

**F3 — Kiki typological explanation sufficient**

If the relevant Kiki motifs are adequately explained by broader mythological typology without local social-memory content, the mythological bridge is weakened.

Node type: Falsifier Node  
Targets: I3, CH1

## 8. Breakthrough Diagnosis

Breakthrough status: **Proceed as a working hypothesis with caution.**

The A-level foundations are relatively stable: paternal-lineage asymmetry, dotaku discontinuity, and the existence of relevant Kiki mythic complexes. The B-level contribution is the abductive integration of these observations. The C-level vulnerabilities are the mechanism of social advantage, the strength of the continental migration bridge, and the historical reading of mythic structures. The main X-level risks are wording that implies proof of immigration, conquest, or direct myth-to-site correspondence.

The hypothesis is therefore not ready as a disciplinary proof, but it is suitable as a structured working hypothesis if all C-level claims remain marked as C and all comparative examples are treated as heuristic analogies rather than evidence.
