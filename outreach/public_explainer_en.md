# Epistemic Gap Mapper: Public Explainer

**Epistemic Gap Mapper** is not a tool for deciding whether a frame-challenging hypothesis is true or false. Its purpose is to map how a user's hypothesis relates to target papers, standard narratives, evidence, gaps, exceptions, assumptions, and falsification conditions.

Modern scholarship is highly specialized. Within each discipline, knowledge can accumulate with great precision. But cross-domain contradictions, unexplained exceptions, and narrative lags often fall between fields. A standard narrative may be repeated in textbooks, reviews, public explainers, and AI outputs without being directly supported by a single clear primary paper. Epistemic Gap Mapper is designed to make these epistemic gaps visible.

The skill works by extracting claims from a user's manuscript, identifying target papers and target narratives, and decomposing the argument into nodes: Claim Nodes, Evidence Nodes, Paper Nodes, Narrative Nodes, Gap Nodes, Exception Nodes, Falsifier Nodes, and Method Nodes. It then classifies claims into A/B/C/X tiers:

- **A**: relatively strong claims supported by existing data or widely accepted findings
- **B**: hypothetical claims consistent with current evidence but not decisively proven
- **C**: not-yet-assertable claims that remain speculative but testable
- **X**: overclaims, unsupported claims, or claims that conflict with available evidence

This classification is not a score. It is a breakthrough diagnosis. It helps users see which claims can serve as foundations, which claims can be presented as hypotheses, which claims must remain speculative, and which statements need to be weakened or removed.

Epistemic Gap Mapper does not replace expertise. It makes outsider hypotheses legible to expertise. Specialists can inspect specific nodes and challenge concrete links, rather than dismissing a cross-disciplinary argument as merely strange, broad, or outside the field. Independent researchers, students, journalists, and interdisciplinary thinkers can use it to separate what they can say from what they cannot yet say.

This is especially important in the AI era. AI systems often reproduce high-frequency narratives from their training data. Such narratives may reflect consensus, but they may also reflect repetition rather than direct evidential strength. Epistemic Gap Mapper uses AI not as an authority, but as a structured assistant for separating claims, sources, narratives, gaps, and falsifiers.

The goal is to distinguish between unsupported speculation, AI slop, testable but unproven hypotheses, and genuinely reviewable research questions. It is not a replacement for peer review. It is a pre-review infrastructure for making difficult hypotheses logically examinable.
